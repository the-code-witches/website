# Hero Section — Pain Points & Copy Directions

_All pain points framed as what the visitor actually feels, not what they would write in a brief._
_⭐ marks the pain points strongest for hero copy — most universal, most tied to the Code Witches' specific offer._

---

## Pain points

---

### ⭐ 1. The lone context-carrier

You are the only person who holds the whole picture — the vision, the constraints, the history of decisions, the things that almost happened. Every technical conversation starts from zero. You spend half your time re-briefing people instead of building. When your contractor finishes, the context evaporates. Again.

> *"You're the only one holding the full picture. That's not sustainable."*

Connects to: *"We hold the complexity. The full stack, every dependency. It fits in our heads and we carry it for as long as you need."*

---

### 2. The question behind the question

Specialists answer the question you asked. But the question you're asking is often not quite the right one yet — and nobody flags that until you've built the wrong thing. You need someone who asks what's actually going on before they start solving.

> *"The question you're asking might not be the right one yet."*

Connects to: breadth across domains means having seen enough different systems to spot the mismatch between what someone thinks they need and what will actually solve it.

---

### ⭐3. New territory, no map

You're building something that doesn't have a template. Stack Overflow doesn't cover your specific combination of constraints. AI gives you a plausible-sounding answer that may be completely wrong for your context. You need someone who reasons through genuinely novel problems — not one who retrieves best practices.

> *"You're in new territory. The map doesn't exist yet."*

Connects to: spectral renderers, drone obstacle avoidance rewrites, live election weighting systems — none of these had a tutorial. Reasoning from first principles across domains is the job.

---

### ⭐4. Smart people, conflicting answers

You've talked to three developers and gotten three different architectures. Everyone is confident. Nobody holds enough breadth to see the full trade-off space. You're the one who has to decide — without the context to evaluate who's right.

> *"Everyone has an opinion. Nobody has the full picture."*

Connects to: the tandem model — two people who think together and hold the whole system, not specialists presenting their single angle.

---

### 5. Something doesn't feel right

Your instinct is telling you something is off — with the architecture, the approach, the direction. But you can't articulate it precisely enough to act on it. You need someone to think out loud with, someone who will help you land on what the real thing is — without making you feel like you should already know.

> *"Something doesn't feel right. But you can't name it yet."*

Connects to: the safe space USP — "you can ask the real question, make the real decision." That's when the actual problems come out.

---

### ⭐ 6. You proved it. Now it needs to become real.

You moved fast. You shipped something. You proved the idea works. But now it's fragile in ways you can't fully see, the codebase is built for yesterday's assumptions, and scaling it feels risky. Vibecoded into a corner — and you're not sure what you're sitting on.

> *"You proved the thing. Now it has to become real."*

Connects to: "trying to grow something that was vibecoded into existence and needs to become real" — already in the USP doc.

---

### 7. The integration nobody owns

Your product touches multiple domains — data, infrastructure, UX, external APIs, business logic. Everyone you bring in optimizes their piece. Nobody optimizes the whole. You're the integrator — in every meeting, every handoff, every post-mortem. The gaps are where things break and the blame has nowhere to land.

> *"Everyone owns their piece. Nobody owns the whole thing."*

Connects to: *"We see the whole system and we don't lose the thread."*

---

### ⭐ 8. The AI anxiety

Management wants a strategy. Your team is either racing ahead without structure or paralyzed waiting for clarity. You're supposed to have a clear position — but the landscape changes every week, the hype-to-reality ratio is exhausting, and nobody is telling you what to actually do in your specific context.

> *"Everyone's talking about AI. Nobody's told you what to actually do."*

Connects to: honest positioning — "we work with AI where it genuinely helps, and we'll tell you when it doesn't." That sentence alone is more trustworthy than three pages of AI strategy decks.

---

### 9. The knowledge that lives in heads, not systems

Your most critical knowledge — how the system actually works, why certain decisions were made, what the workarounds are — lives in one or two people. When they leave, it goes with them. The documentation exists but it's wrong or two years old. Onboarding takes months. The bus factor is too high and everyone knows it.

> *"The knowledge that keeps your system running lives in one person's head."*

Connects to: holding complexity means making it portable — building understanding that doesn't evaporate when a person leaves.

---

### 10. The code nobody understands

Fast iteration created fast debt. AI-assisted development made it faster. Now you have a codebase that works, but nobody — including the people who wrote it — fully understands what it does or why. It's a liability dressed as a foundation, and every new feature is a guess.

> *"The codebase works. Nobody knows why."*

Connects to: growing something vibecoded into existence, AI-generated debt — and having the depth to untangle it without burning it down.

---

### ⭐ 11. Tech and business talking past each other

The technical people can't explain the constraints in terms the business understands. The business can't explain what they need in terms the technical people can act on. You're the translator — in every meeting, every requirement, every sprint review. It's a full-time job stacked on top of your actual job.

> *"Your technical team and your business side are having two different conversations."*

Connects to: the tandem model holds both worlds — they've built for film studios and Mittelstand operators, for research teams and bar staff. They speak the languages.

---

### ⭐ 12. The question you're not allowed to ask

You're in the room with technical people. You're smart — but this is their domain. So you ask the questions you're allowed to ask, the ones that won't make you look like you don't know enough to be here. The real question stays unasked. The real problem stays unsolved. This is how it's always been in technical collaboration and it's costing you.

> *"You're asking the questions you feel safe asking. Not the ones you actually have."*

Connects to: *"Nobody should feel judged for not knowing yet. Everyone deserves a safe place to grow, to ask the question they've been sitting on, to say 'I don't understand' without it costing them something."* — this is the core of what makes the Code Witches different and it's a pain point nobody else names.

---

### 13. Too many options, no decision

You've researched the paths. You've talked to people. You've read the docs. There are three reasonable options and they all have trade-offs and the analysis has been running longer than it should — because the cost of getting it wrong feels higher than the cost of waiting. So nothing moves.

> *"You've done the research. You still can't make the call."*

Connects to: the thinking partner who holds the trade-offs with you — so you can actually make the decision you've been sitting on.

---

### 14. You're growing — but into what?

You have traction. Maybe even a team. But the architectural decisions you're making now will determine what's possible in two years — and you're making them under time pressure with incomplete information. The wrong choices won't hurt today. They'll hurt when you can least afford it.

> *"The decisions you're making right now will define what's possible in two years."*

Connects to: long-term complexity holding — not just solving the immediate problem, but carrying the implications forward.

---
