# Services Section — Restructuring Plan

## Core Problem

The section is structured as a project funnel (sparring → deep dive → dev cycle), which implies one-off engagements. The maintainer contract — the actual preferred and most common collaboration model — is invisible. Someone looking for a long-term technical partner cannot find themselves on this page.

---

## Proposed Structure

### 1. Opening: Lead with the relationship model

Replace the current generic capability intro with copy that establishes the long-term partnership as the primary offer. The message: we don't hand off and disappear. We stay in the codebase, we're reachable, we move fast when things break, and we think with you when new problems come up.

The Skyseed line from the projects section — *"that was the beginning, they're still running it, we're still building on it"* — belongs here, not buried three sections later.

### 2. Bullet points: include the maintainer persona

The three current bullets describe problem types (vibe-coded idea, legacy system, AI anxiety). Add or replace one to address the person who already has a working tool and needs someone who will care about keeping it that way. This is the most common entry point for your actual clients and currently has zero representation.

### 3. Reframe the three cards as an on-ramp

Keep sparring, deep dive, and 1 dev cycle — but reposition them visually and textually as *how most long-term partnerships begin*, not as the end product. A label like "most of our ongoing partnerships started with one of these" achieves this without changing the cards themselves dramatically.

This matters because it shifts the implicit contract: the client isn't buying a scoped deliverable, they're starting a relationship. The cards become an entry mechanism, not the offer itself.

### 4. Do not add a "maintainer contract" card

Adding a fourth card risks making it look like a product tier or upsell. The goal is for long-term collaboration to feel like the natural outcome of working with you — something that happens, not something you purchase. It belongs in the framing, not the card grid.

---

## Tone Shift

Current tone: "here's what we can do for you."
Target tone: "here's how we work with people" — with the implicit message that working with you means you stick around.

The words missing from the current section: *trust*, *reachable*, *ongoing*, *still building*. These should appear naturally in the new intro copy.
